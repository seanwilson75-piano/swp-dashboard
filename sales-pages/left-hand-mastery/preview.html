How to know if you need this

  • When an advanced chord comes up, you can't figure out what your left hand should play.
  • Octaves feel easier, so you fall back on them when a song gets hard.
  • You're not sure when to play the bass note and when to leave it out.
  • You don't know where on the keyboard your left hand belongs.
  • Your right hand is right, but the whole thing still sounds off.

You never learned what to play in your left hand because you were taught to split the chord: part in your left hand, part in your right.

I teach the opposite. The whole sound of the chord belongs in your left hand alone.

The bass plays a note. Your left hand needs an answer.

My method has two parts.

The Zone tells you where to play.

Do you know what part of the keyboard your left hand needs to be in? If not, the Zone will tell you. Step outside the Zone and it won't sound right, even with the right notes.

The 6 Rules tell you what to play.

Each rule is a job your left hand does in a song. Learn all six, and you'll know which one the moment needs.

1

Heavy Bottom

Octaves

For the big moments, like endings and key parts of a song. Low, full, and strong.

2

Root Support

Root + chord

Sometimes the bass note has to be there. Learn when to keep it, and when to let it go.

3

2-Note Tritone

3rd + b7

Where everyone should start dropping the bass note. It's all you need for passing chords, gospel bumps, shout music, and fast songs.

4

3-Note Tritone

Add one note Builds on Rule 3

Builds on Rule 3. Covers dominant, diminished, and half-diminished chords. You already know two notes. Now you learn one more.

5

Target Chords

Major + minor

Most chords in a song are major or minor. Know exactly what your left hand plays under them, from triads to 7ths and 9ths.

6

Passing Movements

Walk-downs Most advanced

The highest level. Your left hand stops just holding chords and starts making melody and movement.
